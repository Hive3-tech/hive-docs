An add-on is a listing in the Honey Marketplace for something you offer to other Hive3 members: an extra, an integration or a bundled service. Each listing has a banner, a description and an access rule. People open the listing, get access if it needs a purchase, and enable it on their account.

**Who can do this:** any account on any plan, and the owner, admins and moderators of a community. Making an add-on members-only, or selling it by subscription or ticket, needs a [Honey Bee or higher](/start/plans) plan. A Worker Bee add-on is public. Each account can have up to 100 add-ons.

## Start a new add-on

<Path items={["My Hive", "My Dashboard", "Add Ons", "Create Add-on"]} />

Open the dashboard of the account that is offering the add-on and select the <UI>Add Ons</UI> card. For a community, open [its dashboard](/create/your-dashboard) first. The <UI>Add-ons</UI> page opens with an <UI>Available Add-ons</UI> section and a <UI>Create Add-on</UI> button.

The form is titled <UI>Create Add-on</UI>. A panel on the right shows its two steps, a preview card and the <UI>Back</UI> and <UI>Next</UI> buttons. Unlike events and courses, add-ons are not saved as drafts, so finish the form in one sitting.

## Basic Info

<Steps>
<Step title="Add an Add-on Banner">
Optional. Upload a wide image; 1200 × 400 pixels is recommended.
</Step>
<Step title="Enter the Title">
Required. Up to 80 characters.
</Step>
<Step title="Write the Short Description">
Required. Up to 160 characters. It appears on the marketplace card.
</Step>
<Step title="Add an External URL, if you have one">
Optional. The field's hint is "Optional link to the add-on, docs, or install page." Hive3 stores the link with your add-on, but the add-on page does not display it, so put any link readers need in the description as well.
</Step>
<Step title="Write the Detailed Description">
Required. Use the editor, up to 3,000 words, or select <UI>Upload Document</UI> and choose a PDF or Markdown file. Explain what the add-on does, who it is for and how to get started.
</Step>
<Step title="Choose badges">
Up to 5 badges from the suggestions.
</Step>
</Steps>

If your plan includes credentials, you also see a <UI>Credential image</UI> field. People who enable the add-on see the credential on its page.

## Access

This step is titled <UI>Add-on Access</UI>. Choose how people discover and get access.

<Steps>
<Step title="Choose the visibility">
<UI>Public</UI> lets anyone view the add-on. <UI>Members only</UI> limits it to members or collaborators. <UI>Subscription / ticket</UI> requires a subscription or a one-time ticket purchase. Worker Bee accounts see only <UI>Public</UI>.
</Step>
<Step title="For paid access, connect payments">
Connect Stripe for subscriptions and PayPal for one-time tickets in <UI>Monetize</UI>. See [Get paid](/community/get-paid).
</Step>
<Step title="Pick a plan or set a ticket price">
Choose a plan under <UI>Select subscription</UI>, or turn on <UI>Enable one-time ticket pricing</UI> in the <UI>One-Time Add-on Ticket</UI> section and enter a <UI>Price</UI> and a <UI>Currency</UI> (USD, EUR or GBP). Paid access needs a subscription or a ticket. Owners and admins set prices.
</Step>
</Steps>

If you do not choose paid access, owners and admins see a note that payments can be enabled later by editing the add-on.

## Publish

On the last step the button reads <UI>Publish Add-on</UI>. Select it and Hive3 asks <UI>Ready to publish?</UI>. Choose <UI>Create Add-on</UI> to confirm. Hive3 shows <UI>Add-on created successfully!</UI> and takes you to the add-on page.

## What visitors see

Visitors find your add-on in the <UI>Honey Marketplace</UI> in the sidebar, which has a search box and filters. Opening it shows your banner, title, description and badges, and an <UI>Access</UI> card.

| Situation | Button the visitor sees |
|---|---|
| Free add-on | <UI>Enable Add-on</UI> |
| Needs a subscription or ticket they do not have | <UI>Purchase Add-on</UI>, then <UI>Enable Add-on</UI> once paid |
| Already enabled | <UI>Disable Add-on</UI> |

Once enabled, the card shows <UI>Enabled</UI> and <UI>Renews</UI> dates. You see <UI>You manage this add-on.</UI> on your own add-on, and your account name and avatar identify the owner.

## Edit, hide or archive

On your <UI>Add-ons</UI> page, the add-ons you created have a menu in the top corner of their card.

- <UI>Edit</UI> reopens the form as <UI>Edit Add-on</UI>. The last button reads <UI>Update Add-on</UI>, and the confirmation reads <UI>Ready to update?</UI>. You can also select <UI>Edit Add-on</UI> on the add-on page.
- <UI>Hide from profile</UI> removes the add-on from your public lists and from search without deleting it. Choose <UI>Unhide</UI> to bring it back.
- <UI>Archive</UI> removes it. Add-ons you did not create do not show this menu.

## Tips

- Lead the description with what the add-on does for the person enabling it, not how it is built.
- Start with <UI>Public</UI> and add payment later, once you know what the add-on is worth.
- Use badges for the category people will search for.
- Keep the short description under the limit by describing one benefit.
